The short answer

A war premium, priced out

In February 2026, before the war, the national average was $2.91.6 A U.S.–Iran war that began in late February closed most of the Strait of Hormuz — the passage for about a fifth of the world's oil — and crude ran to $136 a barrel by October 8.1013 Gasoline peaked at $4.56 on May 21 and set a September record of $4.48.54

Since late October, that premium has been coming out — first on diplomacy, then on physical supply. On November 2, WTI fell 7.4% and Brent 7.6% as Washington and Tehran signaled openness to a deal.14 By November 6, Brent had fallen 15% on the week.15 By November 13, the IEA declared the first global oil surplus since the war began.10

Crude did the heavy lifting. Brent is down 34% from its peak to $89.37; WTI settled at $80.17 on November 23, its lowest since August 10.12 Because crude is about half the retail price, that move is worth roughly $1.10 a gallon before taxes and margins.9 The pump has given back 66 cents of it.

The rest of the gap is timing, taxes, distribution, and margin. And the relief is incomplete: gasoline is still 18.4% more expensive than last November, diesel is up 53% year on year, and attacks on Hormuz shipping are at their highest level since the war began.113

The war premium is leaving the pump. The war has not left the Gulf.

Why it fell

Five forces, one direction

The drop is overdetermined: diplomacy, physical supply, weak demand, seasonality, and retail pass-through all pushed the same way in November. Their relative weights are a judgment; the direction is not.

−7.4%WTI, November 2
biggest daily drop since July 27

The risk premium cracked first

On November 2, with U.S. midterms the next day, WTI fell 7.4% and Brent 7.6% after President Trump said Iran wanted a deal and Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Washington preferred diplomacy. The market had spent October pricing in post-midterm escalation; crude gained more than 10% that month.14 When the strike didn't come, the premium had to come out. By November 6, Brent had fallen 15% on the week.15

82%of pre-war Hormuz crude flows
restored by October

The barrels came back

Diplomacy would not have mattered if the oil had stayed trapped. Global supply rose 2.3 million barrels a day in October to 105.4 million, a 5.3 million-barrel-a-day increase since August. Gulf exports excluding Oman reached 18.4 million barrels a day; Hormuz crude flows hit 15 million, or 82% of pre-war levels. Inventories built by 52 million barrels, and the IEA flipped its fourth-quarter balance to a 1.7 million-barrel-a-day surplus.1011 Libya's Sharara pipeline reopened on November 23.12

Demand failed to absorb it

The IEA cut its 2026 demand forecast to 102.3 million barrels a day, down 2.7 million from 2025. High prices accelerated efficiency and electrification; transport-fuel demand will see only "very modest" gains in 2027.10 China's refinery throughput fell 2% month on month and 8.1% year on year in October.11 U.S. gasoline demand was steady at 8.79 million barrels a day — a shoulder-season level, not summer strength.2

81.3 mb/dglobal refinery runs, October
a seasonal low

The seasonal layer

Refiners switched to cheaper winter-blend gasoline after Labor Day, and maintenance season kept runs at a seasonal low of 81.3 million barrels a day in October. The IEA expects runs to rebound to 84.7 million by December.10 AAA flagged the winter-blend transition as a driver as early as October 22.3 More supply, cheaper blend stock, weaker demand: real, but measured in cents, not dollars.

EIA, 2025 average

The station passed it through

Crude is the largest component of the retail price, but taxes, refining, and distribution are not. At 2025 averages, EIA put the split at 51.4% crude, 17.8% refining, 16.6% distribution and marketing, and 14.3% taxes.9 Gasoline's own peak came on September 24, two weeks before Brent's October 8 high, so the final crude spike was never fully reflected at the station. When crude collapsed, retail had room to fall — but not one-for-one. Gasoline futures were $2.396 on November 23, and AAA saw another 15 cents possible by Thanksgiving.162

What happens next

Cheaper, not cheap

Forecasts diverge because the war does. The EIA's November outlook predates the latest slide; the banks are already looking through it to 2027.

EIA (November 10, finalized November 5): Brent averages around $100 in November and December; gasoline averages $3.89 for 2026 and $3.73 for 2027; diesel $5.25 and $5.03; U.S. crude production reaches a record 14.3 million barrels a day in 2027.8

IEA (November 13): surplus in the fourth quarter, larger in 2027, but "attacks on shipping undermine maritime security, which is pivotal to a sustained recovery."10

Banks: Goldman sees Brent averaging $88 in 2027 and $78 by December 2027; JPMorgan sees the high $70s in the first half of 2027; Bank of America warns low inventories leave the market vulnerable.1112

The holiday: AAA expects 73 million road trips. At $3.64, the national average is above the 2022 Thanksgiving record of $3.58 — so a record depends on whether the next few days deliver the 15 cents AAA thinks are possible.2

How this was built

Physical data. IEA Oil Market Report (November 13), EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook (November 10), and EIA weekly petroleum status reports as summarized by AAA.2810

Ranking. Computed from the EIA weekly U.S. regular gasoline series, 1990–2026, using 28-day changes with exact-date matching; 1,878 observations. A "four-week decline" is the change from the same weekday four weeks earlier, not a calendar-month change.6

Estimates. The crude-to-pump pass-through uses the standard 2.4-cents-per-dollar rule of thumb and is labeled as an estimate. It ignores regional basis, taxes, and margin changes. Causal weights are a judgment based on timing and magnitude, not an econometric decomposition; no public dataset isolates those shares precisely.
